The Scenario

Read this before your role play — know the context

Incident — First Access Community Services

You work at First Access as a Support Worker. Two male casual Support Workers don't appear to get along very well. One is Indigenous, named Ryan, and the other is Muslim, named Haider, who has English as an additional language — it is sometimes hard to understand Haider when he gets overwhelmed.


One day their issue escalated and they started to yell at each other. Haider accused Ryan of being lazy and rude, and Ryan made comments about Haider leaving clients to go and pray. Dianne, an Australian-born Support Worker, gets caught up with Ryan and Haider complaining all the time about each other and tries to get them to take sides.


Their conflict impacts the staff and clients. Their shouts could be heard in the office where the Supervisor was in a meeting with a client.


Your role: You will need to intervene between Ryan, Haider and Dianne to diffuse the conflict in a professional manner. After the conflict is resolved, you must approach your Supervisor to debrief.

Character
Ryan
Indigenous Australian Support Worker. Doesn't always make direct eye contact — this is cultural, not rudeness.

Character background

  • Indigenous Australian — indirect eye contact is normal and respectful in his culture
  • Works at his own steady pace — this is not laziness
  • Feels targeted by Haider's comments and is defensive

Your lines

"Look who's talking. The one that leaves everything to go and pray."
"Just take your stupid rug and go pray... who cares if you take extra time off."

Note: Stay in character but don't be over the top. Be grumpy and defensive, not aggressive beyond what's needed.

👨‍🦲
Character
Haider
Muslim Support Worker with English as an additional language. Stutters when overwhelmed or stressed.

Character background

  • EAL/D — English is not his first language
  • Stutters when upset or overwhelmed — portray this gently (no mocking)
  • Needs to pray at set times — this is a religious obligation, not absenteeism
  • Feels dismissed and disrespected by Ryan

Your lines

"He never hurries, even when something urgent happens. I've had enough of him."
"C-c-come on man, l-l-look in me eyes if y-y-you have something t-t-to tell me. What's your p-p-problem?"

Note: The stutter is authentic to the character. Play it with dignity — Haider is frustrated, not incompetent.

Dianne

Character background

Note: If the Support Worker addresses Dianne, back off — her role is to add pressure, not to escalate endlessly.

Scene 2 — You intervene This is assessed

You step in and must handle the situation. There is no script — your approach and professional judgement are what's being assessed.

What you need to demonstrate:
  • Communicate respectfully with all three people
  • Stay professional — don't take sides or judge
  • Make everyone feel included and heard
  • Be patient and sensitive with Haider's communication needs
  • Address Dianne's behaviour too — she's part of the problem
👔

Scene 3 — Supervisor debrief

After the conflict is managed, you approach the teacher (as your Supervisor) and debrief.

Key points to include in your debrief:
  • Describe what happened — without judgement of either party
  • Explain why both Ryan and Haider are valuable to the team
  • Note that Haider's right to pray is protected — the employer could breach anti-discrimination law if he's prevented from practising his religion
  • Suggest at least one strategy (e.g. cultural competency training, team cultural sharing, mediation)

Assessment Criteria

All 6 must be Satisfactory. Tap each to see what the assessor is looking for.

Criterion 1
Respectful communication with diverse colleagues
How you talk to and treat Ryan, Haider and Dianne during the conflict.
✅ Assessor is looking for
  • Patient — doesn't rush or dismiss anyone
  • Calm — no raised voice, no negative facial expressions
  • Allows Ryan and Haider time to re-group and respond
  • Does not mirror or match their agitation
Criterion 2
Maintains professional approach
Staying neutral, non-judgemental, and without taking sides.
✅ Assessor is looking for
  • No discriminatory actions or statements
  • Does not judge either person's cultural background or practices
  • Does not take Ryan's or Haider's side — or join in with Dianne
  • Stays composed even if characters escalate
Criterion 3
Inclusive work practices
Making sure everyone is involved and their needs are considered.
✅ Assessor is looking for
  • Ensures all three people feel heard — including Dianne
  • Considers individual needs (Ryan's eye contact, Haider's language)
  • People's values and identities feel acknowledged, not dismissed
Criterion 4
Effective verbal and non-verbal communication
How your body language and tone supports what you're saying.
✅ Assessor is looking for
  • Appropriate eye contact (aware Ryan may not make direct eye contact)
  • Aware of personal space — not invading anyone's space
  • Calm tone of voice throughout
  • Appropriate gestures — no pointing, no threatening posture
Criterion 5
Sensitive handling of language barriers
How you communicate with Haider given his EAL/D background and stutter.
✅ Assessor is looking for
  • Active listening — doesn't interrupt or rush Haider
  • Repeats or summarises to check understanding
  • Does not raise voice or show frustration at his stutter
  • Uses simple language; may use gesture or imagery if helpful
Criterion 6
Seeks supervisor assistance and debriefs
The supervisor conversation at the end of the role play.
✅ Assessor is looking for
  • Approaches the supervisor (teacher) — doesn't wait to be asked
  • Describes situation non-judgementally
  • Acknowledges the value of both Ryan and Haider
  • Flags employer risk re: Haider's religious practice and anti-discrimination law
  • Suggests at least one practical strategy

AT3B — Written Questions

Complete these after your role play. Your answers save automatically on this device.

1
Identify the issue that caused the conflict between Ryan and Haider.

Think about the cultural and religious factors at play — not just the argument itself.

✓ Saved
What a satisfactory answer covers
  • Ryan's communication style reflects his Indigenous cultural background — indirect eye contact and working at his own pace are cultural norms, not laziness
  • Haider's religion (Islam) requires prayer at set times — this is a religious obligation, not absenteeism
  • Personal biases of both parties create tension and misunderstanding
2
Describe how to show respect to and support diverse colleagues in a workplace.

Give at least one clear example. Think about what you'd actually do differently.

✓ Saved
What a satisfactory answer covers (at least one)
  • Avoid discriminatory actions or behaviours
  • Demonstrate understanding of a person's cultural background and needs
  • Allow space for people to practise their cultural customs
  • Don't participate in culturally inappropriate or discriminatory discussions
  • Take action when situations may be unfair or impact someone's rights
3
Describe the benefits of maintaining a professional approach when cultural differences arise at work.

Why does it matter — not just for individuals, but for the whole team and the clients?

✓ Saved
What a satisfactory answer covers (at least one)
  • Role models cultural competence and sensitive practice for the whole team
  • Rapport, empathy and mutual respect lead to calmer outcomes over time
  • Professional behaviour builds trust and confidence, enabling more civil interactions under tension
4
Describe how you can contribute to and promote a culturally safe work environment.

Think beyond this specific scenario — what does this look like day to day?

✓ Saved
What a satisfactory answer covers (at least one)
  • Role model sensitive work practices every day — not just in conflict
  • Be inclusive; consider the impact of diversity before acting
  • Show knowledge of specific cultural needs and customs
  • Display sensitivity and genuine appreciation for cultural difference
5
Describe how you can deal sensitively with language barriers in the workplace.

Think specifically about Haider — what could you do in the moment? What about longer term?

✓ Saved
What a satisfactory answer covers (at least one)
  • Locate a staff member who speaks the same language
  • Identify if an interpreter is needed and arrange one
  • Work respectfully with a family member or carer who is translating
  • Active listening — don't raise voice or show frustration
  • Use imagery or other visual resources
  • Ask for clarification and confirmation of understanding

Tips for the Day

Practical advice to help you feel prepared and confident

🧘
There's no exact script
You're not being judged on your exact words. You're being assessed on your approach, your tone, and your professional judgement. Calm is more important than perfect.
⏸️
Pausing is a skill
If you feel stuck, it's okay to say "I need a moment to think about the best way to handle this." That's what a real professional does — it shows self-awareness.
👀
Watch your body language
The assessor is watching your non-verbal communication as much as what you say. Stay open, avoid crossing your arms, don't point, and keep a calm, steady tone.
🤝
Don't forget Dianne
Many students focus on Ryan and Haider and forget to address Dianne, who is actively fuelling the conflict. Make sure all three people are included in your intervention.
👔
End with the supervisor
The supervisor debrief is its own assessment criterion. Don't forget to approach your supervisor (the teacher) at the end. You need to include the legal risk point about Haider's right to pray.
🔄
Not Satisfactory = not the end
You have up to 3 attempts. If a criterion is NS, you'll only need to redo that specific criterion — not the whole scenario. One rough moment doesn't define your outcome.
