What Do We Mean by Diversity? (10 min)

Diversity in community services covers the full range of human difference. You will encounter all of these in your work:

AreaKey characteristics
DisabilitySensory, intellectual, physical, mental health, acquired brain injury
CultureCustoms, social norms, family practices, beliefs
RaceGenetic variation, physical characteristics
EthnicityShared language, religion, customs, place of origin
Religion & SpiritualityBeliefs, practices, ceremonies — not the same thing as each other
Gender & TransgenderGender identity — not the same as biological sex
IntersexBorn with sex characteristics not exclusively male or female
Sexual OrientationLesbian, gay, bisexual, heterosexual
GenerationalDifferent values, expectations and life experiences across generations
Three Key Concepts (20 min)
📝
AT1 Q1 — take notes here. You will need to define and distinguish these three concepts in your knowledge questions.
Cultural Awareness

Understanding your own cultural background and how it differs from others.

In practice: Identify the cultural needs of clients, colleagues and families. Stay curious and open-minded — curiosity is a professional skill.

Cultural Competence

The ability to participate ethically and effectively in intercultural settings.

In practice: Ensure diversity policies are implemented; staff receive appropriate training; diverse communities are consulted in service design.

Cultural Safety

An environment that is spiritually, socially and emotionally safe — where people are not exposed to assault, challenge or rejection because of who they are.

In practice: Be non-judgemental. Use respectful language. Avoid stereotyping. Build trust over time — it cannot be assumed.

💬
Discussion: "Which of these three do you think is hardest to achieve in a community services workplace? Why?"
The Cultural Iceberg (15 min)

Culture is like an iceberg. Most of it is invisible — including to the people who hold it.

Above the waterline (visible)

Food, clothing, language, celebrations, art, greetings, music, dress

Below the waterline (invisible)

Values, beliefs, assumptions, attitudes to authority, concepts of time, gender roles, communication style, notions of fairness, religious beliefs, family roles, approaches to conflict

"Most of what shapes how we see the world is invisible — including to ourselves."

New Learning 2
What Is Unconscious Bias? (10 min)
  • Bias = a tendency to favour or disfavour something based on background, culture, or experience
  • Unconscious = we are not aware it is happening
  • Not about being a bad person — it is a feature of how the brain categorises and pattern-matches to save time
  • Why it matters: unexamined bias directly impacts the quality of care clients receive
Common Types of Bias
TypeWhat it looks like in practice
Affinity biasFavouring people who are similar to us — in background, communication style, values
Confirmation biasSeeking information that confirms what we already believe — ignoring contradicting evidence
Attribution biasExplaining our own mistakes differently from how we explain others' mistakes
Halo / horns effectOne positive or negative trait colours our entire view of a person
Generational biasAssumptions based on age — older workers can be seen as resistant to change; younger workers as inexperienced. It runs in all directions.

Afternoon

Small groups of 3–4 people — mix ages and experience levels deliberately.

  1. Draw an iceberg on large paper — freehand is fine
  2. Above the waterline: things you'd notice about each other in the first 5 minutes
  3. Below the waterline: things that took longer to learn — or that might never be visible without trust
  4. Each group shares one item from below the waterline with the whole class
"This is what we navigate every time we start with a new client, colleague, or family."
Facilitator — Teacher Only Move around the room during the activity. For early finishers: "What assumptions might someone make about your group just from what is above the line?" The variety in the room IS the learning — call it out positively and name it as a professional asset.
