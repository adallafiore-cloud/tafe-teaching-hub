Teacher Scripts

Facilitation Notes

Opening — Duty of care (Slide 31)

"Duty of care is one of those terms that gets used a lot in community services, but people often misunderstand what it actually means legally. It doesn't mean you're responsible for everything that happens to your client. It means you must avoid causing reasonably foreseeable harm through careless behaviour. The key word is 'reasonable.' You're not expected to be psychic or to prevent every possible outcome — you're expected to apply the judgement of a competent professional in your role. If you work within your job description, follow your org's policies, and use common sense, you are very unlikely to breach duty of care."

Confidentiality — when can you disclose? (Slide 37)

"Here's the rule: you do not disclose confidential information without consent — full stop. Two exceptions: one, when it's necessary to protect someone from harm — not possible harm, not theoretical harm, but an identified risk of harm. Two, when formally required by a court of law. That's it. Not because your supervisor asked. Not because the client's family is worried. Not because you think it would be in the client's best interests. Check your org's policy and document your decision-making if you ever face this situation."

Values and assumptions (Slide 41–43)

"I want to talk about something that's easy to say and hard to practise: being non-judgemental. We all have values. We all have assumptions. They shape how we see people before we've even heard their story. An extreme version of an assumption is a stereotype — a fixed set of ideas about a whole group of people based on a single characteristic. (read examples from learner guide) These aren't just abstract moral problems — they directly affect the quality of care we give. If you assume someone who uses drugs is unreliable, you'll treat them differently. If you assume someone experiencing DFV 'just needs to leave,' you'll miss what's actually keeping them there. Our job is to notice these judgements when they arise in us and not let them drive our practice."

Discussion Prompts

Class Discussion Questions

Use as needed throughout the session
  • What's the difference between a policy and a procedure? Can you think of an example of each from a community services setting?
  • If a client tells you they're planning to harm someone, are you required to maintain confidentiality? What would you do?
  • What does 'reasonably foreseeable harm' mean? Give an example where harm was foreseeable and one where it wasn't.
  • Simon buys clothing for a child client out of his own pocket and offers to have her stay at his house. Why is this problematic — even though his intentions are good?
  • What is one assumption you've noticed yourself making about a particular group of people? How might that assumption affect your work as a community services worker?
  • What's the difference between stereotyping and having a culturally informed understanding of a community?
Quiz Answers

Student Quiz — Benchmark Answers

Q1B — Take reasonable care to avoid reasonably foreseeable harm through careless behaviour. Duty of care does not require you to predict all possible harm, only that which a reasonable person could foresee.
Q2C — To protect someone from harm, or when formally required by a court of law. Not supervisor requests, not family requests, not personal judgement about the client's 'best interests.'
Q3B — Working outside work role boundaries. Taking a client child home and buying them gifts creates professional and legal risks regardless of intent. Simon should consult his supervisor and refer to appropriate services.
Q4C — People have strengths and capacity to address their own situations — they are the experts on their own lives. This reflects a strengths-based, empowering approach. Options A and B reflect harmful assumptions.
Differentiation

Supporting Your Cohort

Support strategies
  • Pre-teach key legal vocabulary: duty of care, negligence, mandatory reporting, disclosure
  • Provide the Duty of Care Checklist handout as a reference during activities
  • For EAL/D students: pair for the ethical dilemma activity and allow verbal responses
  • Use the sample welfare check policy (LG p.26–28) as a worked example before activities
Extension strategies
  • Research the actual text of the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) — what does it require?
  • Find one example of a community services worker who was found liable for negligence — what happened?
  • Compare the ACWA Code of Ethics with the NDIS Code of Conduct — key similarities and differences
⚠️
Watch for: Students often confuse 'duty of care' with 'always doing whatever the client wants.' Clarify that client autonomy and duty of care can sometimes be in tension — and that working within your role boundaries is how you navigate this.
