Chapter 4 — Addressing Safety Concerns

Steps for Addressing Safety in a Crisis

Your first task is identifying who is at risk, what the risks are, and how serious they are. These steps don't always happen in this order.

  • 1
    Observe the situation carefully.
  • 2
    Ask direct questions: "Tell me what is happening." "Do you feel safe?" "What would you like to do?" "How can I help?"
  • 3
    Take immediate action if necessary — first aid, remove people from danger.
  • 4
    Active listening — collect information and show empathy.
  • 5
    Help reframe — suggest strategies and help the person identify and express more positive thoughts.
  • 6
    Assess capacity — how able is this person to make decisions right now?
  • 7
    Balance collaboration and direction — collaborate where possible; provide direction when the person is overwhelmed.
  • 8
    Agree on actions — reach agreement about what to do about immediate risks.
  • 9
    Follow duty of care — check and follow org policies and procedures.

Communication Techniques

Active listening: Open body language, eye contact, attentive listening, not interrupting, nodding, minimal utterances ("mm-hmm"), reflecting back what was said.

Affirm agency: "You've been through a lot." "You have options." "You have support." "You've done really well."

Affirm safety: "You're safe here now." "You're in a safe place."

Enable positive thoughts: Help the person identify and express thoughts that build towards a plan, rather than catastrophising.

⚖️
Collaboration vs direction: Always prefer collaboration — it respects the person's agency and builds their capacity. Move to direction only when the person is so overwhelmed they cannot make decisions safely.
Chapter 5 — Self-Care

Taking Care of Yourself

Self-care means taking care of your own physical, mental, and emotional wellbeing as part of your professional practice. You cannot effectively support others if you are not able to support yourself.

Vicarious trauma is the emotional, psychological, and physical impact of being exposed to others' traumatic experiences. It is an unavoidable consequence of working in community services — not a sign of weakness.

Signs you may need support

Emotional numbness or detachment from work · Difficulty sleeping or concentrating · Increased irritability or anxiety · Loss of satisfaction in your work · Physical symptoms (headaches, fatigue)

👁
Supervision is essential when working with people in crisis. Access it regularly — not just when things go wrong. It's part of your duty of care to yourself.
🗓
Critical incident debriefing should happen as soon as possible after an incident — ideally within 3 days. Your org is required to provide this support.
