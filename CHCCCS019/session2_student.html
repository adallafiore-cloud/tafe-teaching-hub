Key Concepts

Legal and Ethical Foundations

Duty of care

A legal principle requiring you to avoid causing reasonably foreseeable harm to others through careless behaviour. In crisis work: recognise and respond to foreseeable risks, work within your role, follow legal requirements.

Privacy & confidentiality

Do NOT disclose confidential information without consent — except to protect someone from harm or if ordered by a court. Always check org policies before disclosing.

Work role boundaries

Working outside your boundaries harms clients and breaches duty of care. If unsure — consult your supervisor and check your job role description. Don't take on tasks you're not qualified for.

Mandatory reporting

In situations involving child safety or DFV, you may be legally required to report. Check your state's legislation (Vic: Children, Youth and Families Act 2005) and your org's procedures.

Codes of practice

Guidelines for good professional practice. ACWA Code of Ethics is the most relevant for community services workers. Sector-specific codes also apply (NDIS, mental health).

Personal values & beliefs

Being non-judgemental is essential. Develop awareness of your own biases, assumptions and stereotypes — they shape how you communicate and treat clients, often subconsciously.

Understanding Negligence

Four Elements of Negligence

For legal action for negligence to be taken, all four elements must be present.

1. Duty of care relationshiptap to reveal

A relationship exists where one person's actions might foreseeably affect the other's safety or wellbeing.

2. Standard of caretap to reveal

A standard for reasonably careful behaviour exists. In professional roles, this standard is higher than for an 'ordinary person.'

3. Breach of dutytap to reveal

The standard of care was NOT met. If you work within your role and follow org policies, you are unlikely to breach duty of care.

4. Harm resultingtap to reveal

Harm must have resulted from the breach. Harm includes physical injury, economic loss, damage to reputation, psychological harm.

Learner Guide Activities

Today's Activities

Activity 2A — Duty of Care (Mary and Joe)

Joe has bipolar disorder, well-controlled by medication. He has joined an alternative religious group whose leader is encouraging him to stop taking his medication. His support worker Mary has noticed significant mood swings. Joe is at risk of risky behaviour during 'highs' and suicidality during depressive periods.

Your reflection — Activity 2A
What is Mary's duty of care here? Does this involve 'reasonably foreseeable harm'? Suggest two things Mary could do to keep Joe safe while respecting his rights.
Activity 2C — Ethical Dilemma (Safi and Martin)

Safi is a support worker in an aged care facility who suspects her new colleague Martin may have been bullying or harming a resident, Lucy, who has dementia. Lucy appears afraid of Martin. Martin has moved between multiple facilities. Safi has no concrete evidence and is unsure whether to report her concerns.

Your reflection — Activity 2C
Who is at risk and how? Where could Safi find guidance? What do you think Safi should do, and why?
