Teacher Scripts

Facilitation Notes

Collaboration vs direction — the key tension in crisis work

"I want to sit with this tension for a moment, because it comes up in every role play. Your default as a community services worker is collaboration — you work with the person, not for them. You ask what they want. You present options. You respect their autonomy. But here's the thing: a person in acute crisis may not be able to make decisions safely. Their cognitive function is impaired. They're overwhelmed. And in that moment, you may need to be more directive — not controlling, but providing a structure the person can't create for themselves right now. The skill is knowing when to move between these modes. And you move back to collaboration as soon as the person's capacity returns."

Vicarious trauma — making it real for students

"Vicarious trauma is not just an occupational hazard — it's an occupational certainty if you work in community services long enough. Every one of you will be affected by this work. The question isn't whether it will happen — it's how you're going to manage it. Research shows that workers who have good self-care practices, strong peer support networks, regular supervision, and organisations that take debriefing seriously are much more resilient to vicarious trauma. The ones who struggle are the ones who think they're supposed to leave it at the office, or who believe that needing support is a sign of weakness. It's the opposite — seeking support is a professional skill."

Zoya and Laura debrief — what to draw out

"In the Zoya and Laura scenario, notice what Zoya does well: she stays calm, she provides first aid, she asks questions, she tells Laura what is going to happen next. But she also checks in about consent — she asks if Laura wants her mother notified, and when Laura says no, she respects that, even in a crisis. That's the professional balance. When you debrief the scenario in groups, ask: at which points was Zoya collaborative? At which points was she directive? Were both appropriate?"

Differentiation

Supporting Your Cohort

Support strategies
  • Self-care plan activity (5A) can be completed verbally with a partner if students struggle with written reflection
  • For students anxious about the role plays, reassure them: they are not expected to be perfect — the assessor is looking for genuine effort and the right instincts
  • Offer additional time to read the role play scenarios before Session 5 (send them now if needed)
  • Vicarious trauma content may be particularly resonant for students with their own trauma histories — be observant and offer space
Extension strategies
  • Research the Australian Association of Social Workers (AASW) Supervision Standards — what does good supervision look like?
  • Find an EAP (Employee Assistance Program) provider used by a community services org — what services do they offer?
