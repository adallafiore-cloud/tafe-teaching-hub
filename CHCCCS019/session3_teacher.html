Teacher Scripts

Facilitation Notes

Stages of Crisis — setting up the Think/Pair/Share

"These six stages are a framework — not a rigid sequence. In real crisis work, you might move backwards through stages, loop, or need to return to emotional support after you thought you'd moved on to planning. The reason the framework is useful is that it gives you a mental map. It stops you from jumping to problem-solving when the person is still in acute distress and can't hear solutions yet. Stage 3 — releasing tension — is often where workers want to rush past. Sitting with someone's pain without trying to fix it immediately is one of the hardest skills in this work."

PFA vs Roberts' Model — clarifying the difference

"Students sometimes confuse these two. PFA is a broad approach — it's what you do in the immediate aftermath of a crisis or traumatic event, often with groups of people. It's less structured and more about immediate stabilisation. Roberts' Seven Stage Model is a more detailed clinical framework for individual crisis intervention — it's what a trained worker might use in a one-on-one conversation with someone in crisis. Both are useful, and in practice you'll draw on both."

Critical incidents — why this matters for YOU (slide 69)

"I want to shift the lens for a moment. We've been talking about crisis from the perspective of the person receiving services. But critical incidents affect workers too — and organisations have a legal and ethical responsibility to support you after a critical incident. Defusing and debriefing are not optional extras. They're part of workplace health and safety. If you ever respond to a serious incident and your employer doesn't offer you any form of debrief, that's a problem — and you're entitled to ask for it."

  • In the Janice and Joe case study (LG p.40), which of Roberts' seven stages can you identify?
  • Why does the learner guide describe crisis intervention as 'first aid for mental health'? What are the limits of that analogy?
  • What's the difference between a direct and an indirect indicator of a safety issue? Which is harder to act on, and why?
  • In the Simon/Alex scenario, Simon locked Alex inside and called an ambulance. Was this the right call? What else could he have done?
  • What do you think a community services worker's responsibility is after they've made a referral? When does their involvement end?
Quiz Answers

Student Quiz — Benchmark Answers

Q1B — Psychological First Aid — providing non-intrusive psychological, social and practical support. Not clinical treatment. PFA listens, provides comfort, connects people to supports, and protects from further harm.
Q2A — Assess lethality (risk of harm) and mental health status. This is Stage 1 in Roberts' model. Many students incorrectly choose B (establishing rapport), but lethality assessment must come first.
Q3B — An indirect indicator. There is no explicit statement of abuse — the behaviour pattern is the clue. Distinguishing direct from indirect is important because indirect signs require 'reading between the lines.'
Q4B — Crisis Assessment and Treatment Team — responds 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. CATT is called for psychosis, self-harm, suicidality, and feeling out of control.
Differentiation

Supporting Your Cohort

Support strategies
  • Provide the Stages and Roberts' Model handouts as references during the Think/Pair/Share — don't expect students to have memorised them
  • For EAL/D students: pair for the case study activity; allow verbal contributions in the debrief
  • The accordion on the student page helps students step through CISM at their own pace
  • If students struggle with 'defusing vs debriefing,' use an analogy: defusing = immediately after a car accident, you check on people. Debriefing = a week later, you all sit down and talk through what happened.
Extension strategies
  • Research the Victorian CATT service — who is eligible? How do you contact them?
  • Compare Roberts' Seven Stage Model with another crisis intervention model (e.g., the Slaikeu model)
  • Read the original Roberts & Ottens paper (2005) — key arguments and critiques
  • Find a community services org in Melbourne that provides critical incident debriefing — what does their process look like?
⚠️
Common confusion: Students often conflate 'critical incident' with 'crisis.' Clarify: all critical incidents trigger a crisis response, but not all crises are critical incidents. A critical incident is a specific, severe event that poses a serious risk to life, health or safety.
