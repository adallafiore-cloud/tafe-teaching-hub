Phase 3
Emotional Breakdown Moment
As you talk about your mother's ongoing struggles, the emotion catches up with you. This is Bella's most vulnerable point in the role-play.
😢
Emotional State: Visibly upset and surprised by your own emotional reaction. Distressed but not in crisis.
Deliver with emotion — these are the key dramatic moments
✓
"My stepfather was arrested, but my mother has never really recovered properly."
Put your face into your hands here — show genuine distress
✓
"Mum has been in and out of a mental health facility. Unfortunately, she's interstate. Otherwise, I'd have her access our services here. My sister is close to her, though."
Get visibly upset — voice may waver, look away briefly
✓
"Sorry, I didn't expect all of these emotions to bubble to the surface now. I love supporting others, but I'm not very good at talking about my own stuff!"
Collect yourself — slight embarrassed laugh, then compose
What to watch for from Estelle here

Estelle should respond with empathy, give you space, not rush past the emotion. This is where "responding to disclosures" and "developing rapport" criteria are most visible to the assessor.

Phase 4
Discussing Vicarious Trauma
After the emotional moment, Bella shifts into a more professional mode. She reflects on how vicarious trauma affects staff at First Access — and why it matters.
💼
Emotional State: Calmer now — thoughtful and professional. Still emotionally present but grounded.
Professional reflection — composed and considered
✓
"You mentioned vicarious trauma. I believe it is so important to have strategies in place for staff here. Not only because of what we do, but because many staff members may be carrying their own personal trauma."
✓
"I think educating staff on how vicarious trauma can be quite insidious and really affect mental health needs to be addressed."
Speak with genuine conviction here — this topic matters to Bella
Key point for Estelle to draw out

Estelle should collaboratively explore the impact of vicarious trauma. They should acknowledge Bella's insight as a person with lived experience. This covers the criterion: "Collaborate to identify the impact of trauma and vicarious trauma in developing mental health issues."

Phase 5
Policy & Practice Feedback
Estelle asks Bella for feedback on First Access' trauma-informed policies and practices. Bella shares honest observations from her frontline experience — including gaps.
📋
Emotional State: Calm and engaged — Bella is thoughtful, balanced and constructive.
Feedback on policies and procedures
✓
"I think our policies and procedures are very good at considering the needs of clients and staff..."
✓
"...however, I'm not quite sure how we deal with emergency situations when a client is 'acting out'. That one is tricky."
Pause and look thoughtful here — it's a genuine concern
✓
"A policy and procedure needs to be created specifically for trauma-informed care. I don't think we have one."
Feedback on current practices
✓
"As for practices, I definitely think we should have gender considered for programs and support. There is a need for same-sex group programs and a need for LGBTQIA+ programs."
Speak from personal conviction — Bella cares about this
Phase 6
Change Strategies & Closing
Bella and Estelle collaborate on how to strengthen First Access' trauma-informed practice. Bella wraps up by committing to help drive change.
🌱
Emotional State: Hopeful and motivated — Bella ends on a constructive, forward-looking note.
Ideas for change — collaborative and energised
✓

✓
"To get everyone on board, we do need to stress that we are already using a lot of trauma-informed care with our clients. It's just understanding why we do some of the things we do."
Show enthusiasm — Bella sees this as achievable
✓
