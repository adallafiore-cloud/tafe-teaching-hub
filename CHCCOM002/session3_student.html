Part 1 — Key Concepts
📋 Types of Meetings, Agendas & Minutes

Before you can facilitate a meeting, you need to understand what kind of meeting it is, why it needs an agenda, and what a record of it looks like.

Concept 1
Types of Meetings
Formal — AGMs, board meetings. Structured, agenda required, minutes recorded, quorum needed.

Informal — team check-ins, case conferences. Flexible but still purposeful — they have a goal and someone is responsible for keeping things on track.

Online — Zoom, Teams. Same principles as in-person, different logistics. Camera norms, muting, managing participation, handling tech issues all need deliberate attention.
"Every meeting needs a purpose. If you can't say what the meeting is FOR, it probably shouldn't be a meeting."
Concept 2
The Agenda
A written plan for the meeting, distributed in advance so people can prepare. A good agenda includes:

• Date, time, and venue
• List of attendees and apologies
• Agenda items with time allocations
• AOB (Any Other Business) at the end

An agenda keeps the meeting focused. Without one, meetings drift, run over time, and people leave unsure what was decided.
"An agenda isn't bureaucracy — it's respect for everyone's time."
Concept 3
Meeting Minutes
A formal written record of what happened in the meeting. Good minutes record:

• Who attended (and who sent apologies)
• What was discussed (summary, not transcript)
• What was decided
• Who is responsible for each action item and by when
• Date and time of the next meeting

Minutes are a legal record. They must be factual, objective, and distributed promptly after the meeting.
"What gets written in the minutes is what officially happened."
Remote meeting tips

Online meetings need extra structure. Agree on camera norms before you start. Assign someone to manage the chat. Use the 'raise hand' feature so everyone gets a turn. Have a backup plan for tech failures. The same principles — agenda, facilitation, minutes — apply exactly as they do in person.

Part 2 — Group Dynamics

  
👥 Tuckman's Stages of Group Development


  
Every group goes through stages as it develops. Understanding where your team is helps you respond as a facilitator — and explain what you're seeing as a worker.


  

    

      
🌱

      
Forming

      
Group comes together. Polite, unsure, getting to know each other. Roles are unclear.

      
Eg: First session with a new team at a community services organisation.

    

    

      
⚡

      
Storming

      
Roles and ideas clash. Tension, conflict, testing boundaries. Normal — and necessary.

      
Eg: Team disagrees on the right approach to case management for a client.

    

    

      
🤝

      
Norming

      
Group settles into agreed ways of working. Trust builds. Roles become clearer.

      
Eg: Team develops shared protocols for how they communicate with clients.

    

    

      
🚀

      
Performing

      
Group is highly effective. Focused on goals, collaborative, handles challenges well.

      
Eg: Team smoothly manages complex cases together with minimal conflict.

    

  


  

    
As a facilitator — read the room

    
If your team is in storming, don't panic. Your job is to hold the space — acknowledge the tension, redirect to the agenda, and draw out different perspectives calmly. Conflict handled well leads to norming. Conflict avoided just delays it.

  


  

    
PREP structure for presentations

    
When you need to present a point of view clearly in a meeting, use PREP:

    
    
      
  • Point — state your main point
    • 
      
  • Reason — explain why
    • 
      
  • Example — give a specific example
    • 
      
  • Point — restate your point to finish
    • 
    

    
Example: "I think we should hold the meeting online [Point] because several team members are working from home that day [Reason]. Last month we used Zoom successfully for our case conference [Example]. Online would be the most inclusive option [Point]."

Key Vocabulary

  
📖 Glossary


  

    
Agenda

    
A written plan for a meeting, distributed in advance. Lists items to be discussed with time allocations.

    
In community services: "The team leader emailed the agenda two days before the case conference."

  

  

    
Minutes

    
A formal, written record of a meeting — who attended, what was discussed, what was decided, and who is responsible for each action.

    
In community services: "The minutes confirmed that Priya would follow up with the client by Friday."

  

  

    
Facilitator

    
The person who manages the flow of a meeting — keeps it on track, on time, and ensures everyone has the opportunity to contribute.

    
In community services: "As team leader, Alex facilitated the monthly case review meeting."

  

  

    
Quorum

    
The minimum number of people required to be present for a meeting to be officially valid and decisions to be binding.

    
In community services: "The board meeting could not proceed because they did not have a quorum."

  

  

    
AOB (Any Other Business)

    
A standing item at the end of a meeting agenda where attendees can raise matters not already on the agenda.

    
In community services: "Under AOB, the team mentioned the upcoming training day."

  

  

    
Tuckman's Stages

    
A model describing how groups develop through four stages: Forming, Storming, Norming, and Performing. Developed by Bruce Tuckman in 1965.

  

  

    
Forming

    
The first stage — the group is new, members are polite and cautious, roles are unclear.

  

  

    
Storming

    
The second stage — conflict and tension emerge as members assert ideas and test roles. Normal and necessary.

  

  

    
Norming

    
The third stage — the group establishes agreed ways of working. Trust develops and productivity increases.

  

  

    
Performing

    
The fourth stage — the group is cohesive, effective, and focused on shared goals.

  

  

    
Accessibility (meeting context)

    
Ensuring meetings are inclusive — considering location, timing, format, language, and participation methods so all members can fully contribute.

    
In community services: "The meeting was held online to ensure the team member on medical leave could still attend."
