AT4 Part A
Conflict Resolution — "Love at First Access"
You arrive at First Access reception to find two clients in a heated argument. Your job: slow things down and find a path forward.
The Scenario
📋Situation Brief
What Has Happened

You're a support worker at First Access. You arrive at the reception area to find:

  • Marcus — loud, agitated. He believes Diane shared his personal housing information with another client.
  • Diane — defensive, denying it. She says she never discussed Marcus with anyone.
Your Job

Step in calmly. Make both people feel heard. Work toward a resolution without taking sides. Know when to involve your supervisor.

⚡ De-Escalation Quick Reference
  • 1Lower your voice — don't match their energy. Calm is contagious.
  • 2Slow your pace — take a beat before you respond.
  • 3Open body language — face both people, arms uncrossed, stay grounded.
  • 4Name the emotion — "I can see this is really frustrating for you."
  • 5Avoid 'you' blame statements — say "what happened" not "what you did".
  • 6Give space — don't rush to fix it. Let people finish speaking.
  • 7Summarise what you heard — show both people you were listening.

AT4 Part B
Interpreter Facilitation — "Accessing an Interpreter"
You meet new client Amara using a professional phone interpreter. Your job: conduct the intake process with cultural safety and correct protocols.
The Scenario
📋Situation Brief
What's Happening

Amara has recently arrived in Australia. She speaks limited English — her first language is Somali. A professional interpreter is joining by phone.

Meeting purpose: explain the First Access intake process and gather Amara's support needs.

Your job: run the meeting using correct interpreter protocols. Speak to Amara, not the interpreter. Keep it clear, respectful and culturally safe.

My Prep Notes — Part B
💾 Saved automatically
📋 Interpreter Protocol — Quick Reference Card
  • 1Introduce yourself AND the interpreter to the client at the start of the meeting.
  • 2Always speak TO the client — not to the interpreter. Say "Amara, can you tell me..." not "Can you ask her..."
  • 3Short, clear sentences — one sentence at a time, then pause for interpretation.
  • 4No jargon or idioms — avoid phrases like "touch base", "ballpark", or "in the loop".
  • 5Check understanding regularly — "Can you ask Amara if she has any questions?"
  • 6Eye contact with the client — look at Amara, not the phone.
  • 7Allow extra time — interpreted meetings take longer. That is normal and okay.

Amara looks confused. You:
a) Repeat it louder
❌ Volume doesn't help understanding. Simplify your language and check in with the interpreter.

✅ Correct! Check in, then rephrase using simpler, shorter words.

❌ Too long — the interpreter cannot accurately remember that much. One sentence at a time.
