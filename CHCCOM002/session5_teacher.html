AT3 Assessment Summary

Part A: Team meeting role play — observed and assessed against marking guide criteria. Each student plays one of five roles: Facilitator, Note-Taker, Team Member A, B, or C.

Part B: Written feedback to 3 group members — specific, constructive, professional. Due end of day.

Part C: Written reflection on the meeting and feedback received — 5 questions. Due end of day.

9:30 — 10:30
Briefing and Group Preparation
60 minutes
🎤 Opening the Day — Say This
How to open (exact script):
"Good morning everyone. Today is assessment day for AT3 — that means the entire day is about Part A, B, and C. Before I explain what we're doing, I want to say this clearly: the role play assesses HOW you communicate, not whether you get every word exactly right. Brief notes in your hand are fine. Your job is to show up professionally, contribute to the discussion, and treat your colleagues with respect. That's it. You've done this in practice — today is just doing it for real."
Explaining the Scenario

Briefly explain the First Access context. Students should already know this from AT2, but a 2-minute recap helps everyone get into the right headspace.

Say:
"You'll remember client Cherie from AT2. After that incident, First Access has formed a new Safety and Respect team. Today is the first meeting of that team. Your role is to be a member of that team. You've been given a role card — open it and read it carefully. You have about 20 minutes to prepare."

Steps during the 60-minute briefing block:

  1. Distribute role cards (5 minutes)
  2. Students read their roles and scripts individually — 10 minutes of silent prep
  3. Pair-share: students briefly discuss with a partner what their character will say — 10 minutes
  4. Whole-group: run through the agenda together so everyone knows the structure — 5 minutes
  5. Students complete the Activities 1-3 and the pre-assessment checklist on the student page — 20 minutes
  6. Final 10 minutes: answer questions, resolve any role confusion
Differentiation — Briefing Block
  • Anxious students: Circulate and say privately: "You've done this in practice. Brief notes are fine. I'm looking for how you communicate, not a perfect performance."
  • CALD students: Before groups form, say to the whole class: "Cultural ways of communicating in meetings — whether that's directness, deference, storytelling — are all valid professional styles. I'm looking for engagement, respect, and clarity. Bring your whole self."
  • Experienced workers: Prompt them to draw on real-world examples in their contributions — it will strengthen their performance and their reflections.
  • Newer students: The Team Member B role is designed for them — validate this explicitly if you've assigned it to a newer student.
Student Anxiety — Pre-Brief Script
If you see a student looking worried, use this line: "The role play assesses HOW you communicate — not whether you get every word right. Brief notes are absolutely fine. Focus on listening and responding professionally. You are ready for this."

Break

Room Setup

During the break: arrange the room so all chairs are around a central table (or a configuration that feels like a meeting). Remove lecturer-at-front setups. The physical environment matters for the role play.

10:45 — 12:00
AT3 Part A — Role Play Observations
75 minutes total — approx. 20-25 min per group
🎤 Introducing the Role Play — Say This
"Okay everyone. We are now in assessment time. I'm going to observe each group run their meeting. You have the agenda — 45 minutes total for the meeting, six items. Facilitator: you're in charge. Everyone else: follow your role. I won't interrupt unless something goes very wrong. I'm here to observe, not to participate. Let's begin."
Observation Protocol
  • Sit to the side — not at the table. Observe one group at a time.
  • Use the Observer Checklist below during the role play.
  • Do not correct students mid-role-play. Note it and address in feedback.
  • If a student completely freezes, give a quiet nod or say "Take your time" — do not prompt their script.
  • If the group finishes early, prompt the Facilitator: "Is there anything the group wants to revisit before closing?"

If a Student Struggles During the Role Play
  • If they freeze: quiet nod, smile, say "Take your time" — do not supply words.
  • If they break character entirely: wait. If they can't recover, note it and address after — do not stop the group.
  • If the Facilitator loses control of the meeting: observe how they recover — this IS the assessment. Only intervene if the group has completely stopped.

12:45 — 1:15
AT3 Part B — Peer Feedback
30 minutes
🎤 Introducing Part B — Say This
"Welcome back. Now we move to Part B — written feedback for three of your group members. You have 30 minutes. The feedback needs to be specific, constructive, and professional. That means: name something they actually did or said in the meeting, give one genuine strength, and one improvement suggestion that is actionable. Write in full sentences. You are not being marked on how positive or negative you are — you are being marked on whether your feedback is useful and professional."
What to Circulate For
  • Feedback that is too vague — prompt: "Can you give a specific example from the meeting?"
  • Feedback that is personal rather than behavioural — redirect: "Focus on what they said or did, not who they are."
  • Students who are stuck on the improvement section — prompt: "What's one thing that would have made their contribution even stronger?"
CALD / LLN Note — Part B
Some students from cultural backgrounds with indirect communication norms may struggle to write critical feedback even when it's constructive. Normalise it: "In Australian workplaces, giving a colleague one area for improvement is a sign of respect. It shows you believe they can grow. That's the professional expectation here."
1:15 — 2:00
AT3 Part C — Written Reflection
45 minutes
🎤 Introducing Part C — Say This
"Part C is your written reflection. There are five questions on the student page — they're also on the printed sheet. You have 45 minutes. A strong reflection doesn't just describe what happened — it analyses it. Why did things go the way they did? What would you do differently? Use specific examples from today's meeting. Full sentences please."
Reflection Question Guidance — What you're looking for
  • Q1 (meeting effectiveness): Students should use evidence — did they achieve the agenda? Were decisions made? Were all voices heard?
  • Q2 (own contribution): Students should name 2-3 specific things they said — not "I contributed" but "I raised the issue of disclosure processes" or "I asked about the reporting procedure."
  • Q3 (feedback received): Students should reference the feedback from Part B and engage with it honestly — agree, partially agree, or respectfully disagree with a reason.
  • Q4 (what differently): Should name a specific skill or a specific moment in the meeting they'd handle differently.
  • Q5 (concept from Sessions 1-4): Any valid concept is acceptable — person-centred approach, active listening, barriers to communication, assertive communication, professional tone.
If a Student Finishes Part C Early
"Use the remaining time to finalise AT1 and AT2 — they're due [confirm date]. I'll be circulating if you have questions."

Group Discussion and Wrap-Up
15 minutes
🎤 Opening the Discussion — Say This
"Before we close, I want to take 10 minutes to debrief together — not about marks, but about the experience. I'll ask a few questions. There are no wrong answers. This is about reflection as a professional."
Discussion Prompts
  • "What was the hardest part of today — and why do you think that was?"
  • "What did you notice about the group dynamics in your meeting?"
  • "If you had to pick one communication skill that made the biggest difference today, what was it?"
  • "What would you do differently as a professional in a real team meeting?"
🎤 Closing the Day — Say This
"Today you did something genuinely difficult — you put yourself in a role and communicated professionally under assessment conditions. That is a real skill that you will use in every team you are ever part of. The work you did today in the role play, in your feedback, and in your reflection is exactly what good community services workers do: they communicate, they reflect, and they keep improving. I'm proud of what you did today. Next session we'll be looking at [Session 6 theme]. Have a good week."
