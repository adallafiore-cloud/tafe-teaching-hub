The Scenario
🏠First Access Community Services
Background

You work at First Access, a community services organisation. After an incident involving client Cherie (which you explored in AT2), leadership has formed the Safety and Respect Team.

Today is the first meeting of this new team. The purpose is to identify gaps in current practice and agree on actions to improve safety and respect for clients and workers.

Meeting Agenda
  1. Welcome and introductions — 5 min
  2. Purpose of the Safety and Respect team — 5 min
  3. Current gaps in practice — 15 min
  4. Proposed actions — 10 min
  5. Measuring improvement — 5 min
  6. Next meeting and close — 5 min

🎤 Facilitator — runs the meeting ►
Facilitator

Character: You are a team leader at First Access. You called this first meeting and volunteered to facilitate. You are organised, calm, and committed to making sure every voice is heard.

3 Things You MUST Do (assessed)
  • Open the meeting professionally and introduce the agenda clearly
  • Actively draw out quieter participants — use their names, ask direct questions
  • Summarise key decisions and close the meeting with agreed actions
Opening Script
"Good morning everyone, welcome to the first meeting of the Safety and Respect team at First Access. My name is [your name] and I'll be facilitating today. We have [names] present — thank you all for being here. I've circulated the agenda — does everyone have a copy? Great. Today our purpose is clear: to start building a safer, more respectful environment for our clients and our team. Let's begin with item one — welcome and introductions."
Transition Lines
"Thanks [name], that's a really important point. Does anyone want to add to that before we move on?"

"We're coming up on time — let me summarise: [your summary]. Moving to item [number]."

"I want to make sure [quiet person's name] gets a chance to add their thoughts — [name], what do you think?"
Closing Script
"That brings us to the end of today's agenda. Thank you everyone — this was a productive first meeting. Agreed actions: [list them]. Next meeting: [proposed date]. Meeting closed."
📝 Note-Taker — records the minutes ►
Note-Taker

Character: You are a senior administration worker at First Access. You have been asked to record official minutes. You are thorough and professional, and you make sure the record accurately reflects decisions and actions.

3 Things You MUST Do (assessed)
  • Record key discussion points for every agenda item using the Digital Minutes Template below
  • Record every decision and every action item, including the responsible person
  • Ask at least one clarifying question during the meeting to confirm accuracy
Important

You do not have required speaking lines — your minutes form is your main assessment artefact for Part A. Use the Digital Minutes Template section below. When you miss something, say: "Sorry — could you repeat the agreed action? I want to make sure I record it correctly."

👤 Team Member A — support worker, 2 years ►
Team Member A

Character: You have been a support worker at First Access for two years. You work directly with clients daily and care deeply about their safety. You are confident, articulate, and focused on practical improvements.

3 Things You MUST Do (assessed)
  • Make at least one substantive contribution about client safety — focus on disclosure processes
  • Build on another team member's point — extend it, don't just repeat it
  • Use person-centred language throughout (the client's rights and experience are central)
Opening Contribution
"Thanks for having us. From my experience working directly with clients, I think the biggest gap is around disclosures — clients don't always feel safe coming forward. I'd like to see a clearer, more visible process for that."
Follow-Up Line
"Building on that — I think clients need to know their rights before an incident happens, not after. Could we include a safety briefing in every intake appointment?"
👤 Team Member B — new worker, 6 months ►
Team Member B

Character: You started at First Access six months ago. You are enthusiastic and observant. Because you are new, you notice things experienced workers take for granted. You bring a genuine fresh perspective and you are not afraid to say "I didn't know that."

3 Things You MUST Do (assessed)
  • Raise the issue of induction and training gaps — what you were not shown when you started
  • Ask at least one genuine clarifying question about the current reporting process
  • Respond to feedback or comments from others professionally — don't just go quiet
Opening Contribution
"I'm fairly new here, so I'm seeing this with fresh eyes. When I started, I wasn't sure what to do if a client raised a safety concern. The induction covered policies but not the practical steps. I think that's a gap."
Your Question
"Can I ask — what's the current process for reporting a safety concern? I want to make sure I'm doing it right."
👤 Team Member C — experienced worker, 8 years ►
Team Member C

Character: You have worked at First Access for eight years. You have seen many initiatives come and go. You are direct, practical, and a little sceptical — but you genuinely care about real change. You push for accountability: named people, deadlines, and simplified systems.

3 Things You MUST Do (assessed)
  • Raise the issue of accountability — insist that action items have a named person and a deadline
  • Advocate clearly for a simplified, plain-language reporting procedure
  • Stay professional even when being direct — assertive, not aggressive
Opening Contribution
"I've been here eight years and I've seen this conversation before. What usually happens: we identify gaps, write a policy, then six months later nothing's changed because no one owns the actions. This time I want named responsibility and clear deadlines."
Advocacy Point
"Specifically — the reporting procedure needs to be simplified. Three pages of forms is a barrier. Workers skip it. One page, plain language. That's what I'm asking for."

Activity 2
📚Communication Skills You Need Today

Six skills you will use in the role play. Tap each card to flip it and see how to use the skill in the meeting.

Skill
Active Listening
tap to flip
Nod, make eye contact, and paraphrase what others say. Don't prepare your next line while someone else speaks.
"So what you're saying is the intake process doesn't include a safety briefing — is that right?"
Skill
Open Questions
tap to flip
Ask questions that can't be answered with yes or no. They open up discussion and invite others to share their thinking.
"What do you think is driving that gap?" or "How could we make the reporting process easier?"
Skill
Summarising
tap to flip
Pull together key points to show you've listened and help the group move forward. Especially important for the Facilitator.
"So we've heard three main gaps: disclosure process, induction, and the reporting form. Let's move to actions."
Skill
Assertive Communication
tap to flip
Say what you think clearly and directly, without being aggressive or passive. Use "I" statements and focus on the issue, not the person.
"I believe the reporting form needs to be simplified — three pages is a barrier for workers."
Skill
Professional Tone
tap to flip
Formal but warm. Avoid slang. Stay calm even when discussing difficult topics. Show respect for colleagues even when you disagree.
"I understand your concern — I'd like to suggest we look at this from a different angle."
Skill
Giving Feedback
tap to flip
In Part B, feedback must be specific, constructive, and professional. Name a behaviour — not a personality. One strength + one improvement.
"You clearly raised the induction gap — a specific example from your own experience would have made it even stronger."

AT3 Part B
💬Feedback to Group Members

After the role play, write structured feedback for three of your group members. Be specific, constructive, and professional. Name what they did — not who they are as a person.

What Makes Good Feedback?
  • Specific — refer to something they actually said or did in the meeting
  • Constructive — the improvement suggestion must be actionable
  • Professional — collegial tone, not critical or personal

AT3 Part C
🤔Reflection Questions

Answer all five questions in full sentences. Your answers save automatically.

What makes a strong reflection?

Don't just describe what happened — analyse it. Why did it happen? What does it mean for your practice? What would you do differently? Use specific evidence from today's meeting.

Q1. How effective was the meeting overall? What evidence supports your answer?
Q2. What was YOUR contribution to the meeting? Give 2-3 specific examples.
Q3. What feedback did you receive from others? Do you agree with it? Why or why not?
Q4. What would you do differently if you ran or participated in this meeting again?
Q5. Which concept from Sessions 1-4 did you see clearly in action today? Give a specific example from the meeting.
